# Slice 36D Decision — Action-Like Signals Do Not Become Invented Grammar Operators

## Decision

Slice 36D shall preserve an exact action-like surface form as an unresolved structural signal when a named deterministic rule matches it. It shall not assign an action-root, predicate frame, participant role, capability binding, permission, route, or action.

## Reason

RMC Language Law Section 39 permits bounded candidate structural analysis and provisional action-like signals. The Predicate–Role Frame Registry retains authority over action roots, predicate frames, participant roles and capability-relevant bindings.

The accepted Slice 36C registry contains no authorized generic action-root grammar operator. Mapping `install`, `send`, `delete`, `run`, or another action-like form to an unrelated operator merely to satisfy an example would fabricate architecture.

## Runtime consequence

For `Do not install it.` Slice 36D may produce:

- a candidate `grammar_negation` association over `not`;
- a candidate `grammar_prohibition` association over `Do` plus `not`;
- a candidate `grammar_reference` association over `it`;
- candidate `grammar_boundary` and `fbsc_loop_seal` associations over `.`;
- an unresolved action-like structural signal over `install`.

The `install` signal explicitly records that Document 5 action-root and predicate-role support is missing. It creates no operator occurrence, capability, permission, route or action.

## Future authority

A later predicate-role slice may consume this source-linked signal only through its own approved, versioned rules. It may not treat the existence of the signal as proof of selected action meaning or authorization.
